Through the year

Major festivals

Lunar dates are converted to the next calendar date, with the nearest festival first.

  1. Lunar month 1, day 1

    Lunar New Year

    Bells at midnight on New Year’s Eve, blessing branches on the first morning.

  2. Lunar month 1, day 15

    First full moon of the year

    New-year prayers for peace; the busiest day of the year.

  3. Lunar month 2, day 19

    Avalokiteshvara Day

    Chanting of the Universal Gate chapter.

  4. Lunar month 4, day 15

    Vesak, the Buddha’s birthday

    Bathing the Buddha, lotus lantern procession, floating lanterns.

  5. Lunar month 7, day 15

    Ullambana, honouring parents

    A rose for each parent and memorial prayers for ancestors.

  6. Lunar month 10, day 15

    Tenth-month full moon

    The harvest thanksgiving festival.

  7. Lunar month 11, day 17

    Amitabha Day

    Day-long recitation and candles in the evening.

  8. Lunar month 12, day 8

    Bodhi Day

    Marking the Buddha’s awakening with sweet milk porridge.

Prayers and retreats

Choose the ceremony and date and list the names to pray for. The temple will get back to you to confirm.

  • Prayers for peace

    Prayers for family wellbeing, the sick, the new year or a long journey. Give each person’s name and birth year.

  • Memorial prayers

    Chanting for the departed: weekly rites, the 49th and 100th day, anniversaries. Give the name and date of passing.

  • Taking refuge

    The refuge ceremony for those who wish to follow the Dharma, held after the first Sunday retreat of each month.

  • One-day retreat

    Every Sunday: chanting, a Dharma talk, walking meditation and a vegetarian lunch. Sign up so the kitchen can plan.

  • Offering a meal to the Sangha

    Families may offer a midday meal to the monastics; the temple explains the ceremony.

No fees. Offerings are from the heart.

Before you come

Visitor guidelines

  1. Dress modestly: covered shoulders, trousers or skirts below the knee
  2. Keep quiet in the main hall and silence your phone
  3. No photos during ceremonies
  4. Remove shoes before entering the main hall
  5. No meat or alcohol on the temple grounds
  6. Park in the lot in front of the main gate

Good to know

Is there a fee for prayers?

No. Registration is free and offerings are voluntary. You can attend or let the temple chant on your behalf.

How are lunar dates worked out?

The site converts dates using Vietnam time, like a wall calendar. Major festivals are also announced on our Facebook page.

Can visitors enter the main hall?

Yes, outside ceremony times. Please dress modestly, remove shoes and keep quiet.

Is there accommodation for retreats?

One-day retreats don’t stay overnight. Longer retreats are announced separately with shared rooms.
